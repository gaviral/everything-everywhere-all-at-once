# Bricks

A brick is a feature in a folder of its own: a view, a card, an input or a panel. The app finds every folder under `ui/src/bricks/`, so adding a brick is adding a folder: no list, registry or app file to edit. Each can be switched on or off while the app runs, in the Catalog (Apps › Catalog). A builder makes one against one file, `ui/src/bricks/contract.ts`, in the playground (`ui/bricks.html`), without the core, a key or the rest of the app.

## A brick's folder

```
ui/src/bricks/<id>/
  brick.json   what the Catalog shows, and how the brick starts
  index.tsx    the brick: a React component, its default export
  …            anything else it needs (its CSS, helpers), inside the folder
```

| `brick.json` | |
|---|---|
| `id` | The folder's name: lowercase letters, digits and dashes. |
| `name` | What the Catalog calls it: a word or two (24 characters at most). |
| `does` | One line for a person: what it does (90 characters at most). |
| `kind` | `view`, `card`, `input` or `panel` (below). |
| `defaultOn` | On in every browser until someone switches it off. A new brick starts `false`. |
| `demoSafe` | `true` when it's fine to switch on live in front of people: it works on an empty map and a full one, needs no setup, and asks for nothing (the camera, the mic) before a click. The Catalog folds the rest under "Not for the demo". |
| `flag` | Optional. `?<flag>` in the address switches it on for that visit (`?level`). `?bricks=<id>,<id>` does the same for any brick by its id, flag or none. |
| `showsLiveWords` | Optional. `true` when it shows `map.partial` (your words as you say them) itself: while it's on, the dock leaves out its big live line, so the words never show twice. Tiny transcript does. |

Where each kind shows:
- **view**: another way to see the map. Its name joins the Map and Palace switch, and it shows in the map's place, padded clear of the dock as the Palace is. Bubbles is one.
- **card**: a small card in the dock, above the composer, where the unfinished card shows. Its height counts with the dock's cards, so a thought just shown moves clear of it. The template is one.
- **input**: a new way in (gestures, a pedal). It mounts with the page, draws at most a small mark of its own, and acts through `actions`.
- **panel**: floats over the page and places itself. The Level meter is one.

## How the app finds them

`ui/src/bricks-core.ts` reads every `./bricks/*/brick.json` (at once: they're small) and every `./bricks/*/index.tsx` (lazily) with Vite's `import.meta.glob`. Folders whose name starts with `_` are skipped: the template is copied, not run. A brick's code loads only once it's on, so a brick that's off costs the page its `brick.json` and nothing else. A brick whose `brick.json` is wrong is left out with a warning in the console; `scripts/bricks-check.mjs` says the same, in the same words, before a merge.

`ui/src/BrickSlot.tsx` mounts each kind in its place, every brick behind a boundary of its own: one that throws shows "… stopped working" with a Switch it off button, and the rest of the page carries on.

## The contract

`ui/src/bricks/contract.ts` is the only app file a brick imports. Its default export gets `{ map, actions, on, brick }`:
- `map`, the map as it is now, read-only: `threads` (a margin note's carries `anchor`, below), `thoughts`, `active` (your thread), `suggestions`, `reminders` (proposed reminders and events), `apps`, `notes` (why a thought went where it went), `points` (each thread's point count, as its card shows it), `rooms` (each thread's room color), `topics` (below), `positions` (what someone placed by hand on a canvas, by thread or thought id: absolute canvas coordinates of its top-left, by whom, when; nothing for the rest), `speakers` (everyone on the map by the name their thoughts carry, each `{ ai }`: true for an AI participant, the Friend), `me`, `partial` (your words as they're heard, not yet final: what the dock's live line shows while you talk, "" between turns; only yours, and only in the tab whose mic is on), `connected`, `jobs` (the AI helpers' jobs, below). The types are the app's own, from `core.ts`, `positions-core.ts` and `annotate-core.ts`. A new snapshot comes with every change, and with every word heard: the arrays in it stay the same objects, so work kept on them (`useMemo` on `map.thoughts`) isn't redone per word.
  - **Margin notes:** a note is a tangent anchored to words: a thread under the point's thread whose thoughts are the note and its replies, with `anchor: { thought_id, quote }`, the words it's tied to. A view shows it beside those words; the Map shows it as a branch.
  - **✓ Agreed:** a thought the room agreed on will list who in `agreed_by`; the core doesn't say who yet, so none has it.
  - **Jobs:** a helper's work as the core relays it (`core/src/guide.rs`; `ui/src/work-jobs.ts` keeps it): `{ id, title, status, result?, parent?, demo, by, at_ms, started_ms?, ended_ms? }`, `status` being `running`, `done`, `failed` or `waiting` (`result` is what a done job found, why a failed one stopped, the question a waiting one asks). The same `id` again is the same job in a new state; `map.jobs` lists each once, in the order it first came (the newest 60), and a window that joins is replayed them, so a brick switched on late has them all. `started_ms` and `ended_ms` are only what this window saw (the `at_ms` of the first running and the first ending), so a job it met already over has no length; `demo` marks a simulated one, which whatever shows it must say. Jobs belong to the connection, never to the saved map.
  - **Themes:** a theme is a group of top threads that belong together; on screen it's always "theme" (people call one thread a topic), while the code keeps the name `topic`. `thread.topic` is the theme a thread is under, by name (a branch has its top thread's; none until it's sorted), and `topics` lists every theme, `{ name, threads }` (`threads`: how many top threads), in the order they first come among the threads. The core's AI names them (6 at most, two or three plain words) once the map has 4 top threads with points, and sorts each new top thread within a minute (core `topics.rs`); a person's rename or move is theirs, never undone by it. A view that colors by theme takes a theme's place in `topics` as its color, so views agree (Bubbles' six tints).
- `actions`: `addThought(text)` (as if typed in the composer), `openThread(id)` (as a Palace picture does: back to the Map at that thread, highlighted; your place moves there), `select(id)` (that thread is "this" for what's said next, as pointing at its card makes it), `notify(text)` (one line in the notice lane), `position(id, x, y)` (a thread's card or a thought's point placed there for every window, kept within ±1,000,000; both null: back to the view's own layout; call it on every step of a drag: steps go at most once a frame, and your own come back in `positions` with `by` you), `listen(on?)` (a tap on the mic: `true` on, `false` off, nothing switches it; it starts the mic only inside a click or key press in this tab, so a brick never turns it on by itself). Each answers on screen as its button does.
  - `annotate(thoughtId, quote, text)`: a margin note by you on `quote`, words picked in that thought's point, for every window. The first on those words starts their note, a later one answers in it; both come back in `threads` and `thoughts` as placed (no model reads a note). False when it can't go (no words, over 6,000 characters, the core away); the core refuses one whose point left the map, and says so.
  - `openCatalog(id?)`: the Catalog, as Apps' button opens it; with a brick's id, at its row, marked, its switch focused.
  - `hint(text, target?)`: one line (140 characters at most) beside the part named `target` with an arrow at it, as another participant's point shows, or in the corner without one; your brick's name on it, in this window only. `target` is a `data-guide` name: the app's (docs/guide.md) or one the brick gives its own part. False when nothing showed.
  - `renameTopic(from, to)`: theme `from` is called `to` for every window (spaces as one, 40 characters at most); a name already there, in any case, makes the two one. `moveToTopic(threadId, topic)`: that thread's top thread, with its branches, goes under theme `topic` for every window. Both say false when they can't go (no name, the same name, the core away); the core says why when the theme or thread has left the map. In the app they go straight to the core (ui/src/topics-core.ts), so `App.tsx` passes neither; the playground answers them as the core would.
- `on(name, listener)`, or `useBrickEvent(on, name, listener)` in a component:
  - `landed`: a thought just placed, `{ thoughtId, threadId, mine, model, ms, by }`: `model` the model that placed it ("Muse Spark 1.3"; null when a rule did, or this window didn't hear which), `ms` how long placing took, from said to landed (the point's own time), `by` who acted: `{ kind: "brick", id, name }` when a brick's `addThought` added it in this window, else `{ kind: "person", name }`.
  - `words`: your words as they're heard, each time they change (as `map.partial` then); "" once the turn ends or the mic stops.
  - `loudness`: the mic's level, 0 to 1, every 320 ms while it's on.
  - `notice`: a notice or map update as this window shows it, a repeat too: `{ text, kind, time }`, `kind` being `notice`, `error` or `map-change`.
  - `pointing`: what the guide's arrow points at (another participant's point, or a brick's hint), `{ target, by, thoughtId?, threadId? }` (a point's thread too), or null once it's down: a view dims the rest once, never twice.
  - `asked`: something you asked for by voice or typing that a brick answers, only in your windows: `{ what: "notices" }` is "what just happened?". It never lands on the map.
  - `speaking`: in a call, someone took the floor or gave it back, `{ speaker, on, mine }` (yours too, `mine`): the core's word from their live words (`core/src/call.rs`), never the words themselves, which come with the turn's thought as it ends. Alone, never.
- `brick`: its own `brick.json`.

Three conventions come with the page:
- An element marked `data-thread={id}` is "this" while pointed at or focused (`this-card.ts`), so "rename this to …" works on it without calling `select`.
- A brick styles only its own classes, each starting with its id (`.template`, `.template-open`), with the app's tokens (`--bg`, `--card`, `--ink`, `--muted`, `--line`, `--accent`, `--amber`, `--ai`, `--room-<color>`, `--shadow`), so it reads right in light and dark. It never draws a `<header>`: the app's is the page's only one.
- A brick names a part of its own for a hint, or the guide, with `data-guide`, starting with its id (`data-guide="tiny-transcript"`, `"tiny-transcript:size"`); a view marks the map's threads and points with theirs (`thread:<id>`, `point:<id>`), as the Spine does.

The contract grows by a line: a new field or action is added to `contract.ts`, and its source to `bricks-core.ts`, `BrickSlot.tsx` and `App.tsx` (and the playground). Every brick depends on that file, so that's the hub's change, not a builder's.

## Slots

The page's regions, each an element marked `data-slot="<name>"` while it's on screen (`SlotName` and `SLOTS` in `contract.ts`). A brick finds one by name (`document.querySelector('[data-slot="stage"]')`), to draw beside or in it, or keep clear of it. The layout may move a region, or bring one that isn't there yet; the names stay.

| Slot | The region | Today |
|---|---|---|
| `top-left` | the wordmark and the map's name | the header's left (`.brand`) |
| `top-right` | Find, Catalog, people | the header's buttons (`.header-actions`) |
| `bottom-left` | the camera: following, zoom stops | not yet: the Notice timeline's clock and the corner caption place themselves there |
| `bottom-right` | the shelf, and the held stack above it | not yet |
| `stage` | bottom center, with room beside it: the composer, captions, one notice | not yet: today's dock spans the window's width, so a view's corners stay clear of it, at the right (the Spine's) |
| `status` | the status line: the mic, its time, live words, what's waiting | the header's status (`.status`) |
| `rail` | the panel at the right: Talk, History, Catalog, Settings | not yet |
| `shelf` | what you keep at hand | the Spine's shelf, while the Spine is the view |
| `held` | questions waiting for your pause | not yet |
| `slot:<thought id>` | where a thought on its way will land | marked by the view that shows it (the Spine's dashed slot); a flight aims there |

A flight in the air marks its bubble `data-flight="<thought id>"` (Bubble flight), so a view can wait for it to arrive.

## On and off

The Catalog lists every brick by kind, each with its name, its line and its switch; bricks that aren't `demoSafe` fold under "Not for the demo". A switch acts at once and only in this browser: what was switched is kept in `localStorage` (`e2a2o.bricks`), every other brick follows its `defaultOn`, and other windows of the same browser follow along. A view switched off while it's shown hands the window back to the map.

## The playground

`ui/bricks.html?brick=<id>&sample=<name>` mounts one brick as the app does, against a map from `samples/` replayed through the window's own reducer (`sample=empty` for none). Every brick is there, the template too. What the brick asks for is answered as the app would (a thought it adds lands on the thread you're on 1.2 s later, placed by Muse Spark 1.3 as its route says; `openThread` moves your place; a margin note comes back as the core's changes would) and logged on the right, with what it heard under it. "Add a thought", "Talk, then shout" and "Show a notice" fire `landed`, `loudness` and `notice`; "Say a thought" says one: its words come in `map.partial` and `words` one at a time (a word every 250 ms), then, at the turn's end, the thought lands; "The Friend says one" has an AI participant join and say a thought; "Helper jobs" plays a simulated helper's jobs (one running with a side job, one done, one asking, one failed) as the core relays them; "Point at a thread" fires `pointing` (again: null); "Ask what just happened" fires `asked`; "Someone speaks" fires `speaking` (Maya on, then off 1.5 s later); "Core away" sets `map.connected` to false. In the console, `brickActions` is the shown brick's own actions, to try one by hand.
- While building: `cd ui && npm run dev`, then open `http://localhost:5173/bricks.html?brick=<id>`; it reloads on every save.
- Built: `npm run build` builds it after the app, into `ui/dist`, and the core serves it at `/bricks.html` on this laptop.

## A builder's rules

1. Copy the template: `cp -R ui/src/bricks/_template ui/src/bricks/<id>`, then put your id where "template" is, in `brick.json`, `index.tsx` and the CSS (its file name and every class).
2. Stay in your folder. Import only `../contract`, React, Motion and, for a canvas (pan, zoom, drag), React Flow (`@xyflow/react`, MIT, and its `dist/base.css`). A new package, a model or a field in the contract is the hub's call: ask. Runtime models must be Meta's (the hackathon's rule).
3. Start off: `defaultOn: false`. Set `demoSafe: true` only once it works on an empty map and a full one, at a phone's width, and asks for nothing before a click.
4. Work in the playground: a full map, the empty one, a phone's width and dark mode (DevTools emulates both).
5. Done when `node scripts/bricks-check.mjs <id>` passes, after `cd ui && npm run build`.

`scripts/bricks-check.mjs` holds: every folder's `brick.json` (the app's own rule) and default export; imports (only `../contract`, React, Motion, React Flow and the folder's own files; no `<header>`; a `data-guide` name of its own starts with its id); CSS (every selector names one of the brick's classes, keyframes too, and every class styled is used); `contract.ts` takes only types from the app, and its slot names are this page's; each brick mounts in the built playground on a full map, an empty one and at a phone's width, with no error and nothing running off the side. The contract itself, in the playground: `landed` with its model, time and who (a person, or the brick that added it), `words` as they grow, `pointing`, `asked`, `speaking`, a margin note and its reply, `openCatalog`, a hint and one refused; in the app: the slots on today's layout (no stage on today's dock), a margin note from the core tied to its words in the Spine, "what just happened?" opening the Notice timeline (off: a notice says where to switch it on, and the view stays), and the Notice timeline's clock clear of Tiny transcript's line on a phone. Run for all bricks, it also checks the app: at their defaults bricks add nothing to the first screen; the Catalog lists every brick; each view brick, switched on, joins Map and Palace, shows in the map's place and hands back to the map; a switch survives a reload; `?level` still shows the Level meter. A planted breach of each folder rule is caught, so the check can't pass by missing one.

## The bricks so far

- `level` (panel, off): the `?level` meter of the loudness readings the core hears, for setting a volume app's level. Moved here from `Level.tsx` unchanged: `?level` shows it, and without it nothing loads.
- `brick-chip` (panel, off): a small chip naming a brick on each point that lands while it's on, anyone's, first in the point's own tools (left of Delete and Move), so it shows when they do: pointed at, or tapped on a phone. It's meant to name the brick that acted on a thought; `landed`'s `by` now says which, and for now it still names only itself. Kept for the visit; switched off, every chip goes.
- `bubbles` (view, off, demo-safe): every thread as a bubble in a place of its own, sized by its points, floating gently (still under Reduce Motion); a landing ripples its bubble. A bubble opens its thread on the map. Once there are two or more themes, each bubble is tinted by its theme from a fixed set of six (none near the app's blue, amber, purple or red; checked for color blindness), with a legend of themes: pointing at one, or picking it, fades the other bubbles.
- `bubble-flight` (panel, off, demo-safe): your thought lifts out of the box you typed or said it in as a glass bubble, its words where they were; while it's placed it drifts toward the thread you're on, a dotted line ahead to the slot and a comet tail back to the box; placed, it flies to wherever it landed in 400 ms at most (a turn mid-air if that's elsewhere) and settles its words onto the point's: into the view's slot (`data-slot="slot:<thought id>"`, the Spine's dashed slot), onto its point on the Map. `data-flight` marks it while it's in the air, so the view rings the landing once it's in; on the Map the brick rings it. After your first five it slides there only once placed; unplaced after 5 s, it fades where it is. Under Reduce Motion: no flight, the landing's ring alone, and once, at a pause after your first landing, a hint with a switch, Show the flight (kept); every move is drawn from script, so the app's no-motion CSS leaves a flight you asked for alone.
- `notice-timeline` (panel, off, demo-safe): what just happened. Every notice and map update this window shows while it's on, the newest 50, newest first, behind a clock above the dock at the left that's there once the first comes (16 px higher below 576 px wide, clear of Tiny transcript's line); typing or saying "what just happened?" opens the list too (the core's command: `asked`, never a point). Esc, × or a click outside closes it.
- `countdown` (panel, off, demo-safe): your deadlines, nearest first, in a card at the bottom left, beside the Notice timeline's clock: "Feature freeze in 21 h 14 m", the next one under it, amber under three hours, past ones gone, "No deadlines" and Add when none are left. A click or Enter opens an editor upward from it (a label and a date and time each; Add, Remove; Esc, × or a click outside closes it); every change is kept in this browser (`e2a2o.countdown`), and it works when storage throws. A phone shows the nearest only, on one line, the label cut before its time.
- `spine` (view, off, demo-safe): the map as one canvas without end (React Flow). Each top thread is a spine of its points, oldest at the top; a tangent hangs beside the point it came from, as a card with its latest point and a recap line, alternating right and left, a tangent's tangents further out; spines stand apart in six columns; once the AI has sorted the map under two or more themes, each theme's spines stand in a group of columns under a header with its name (double-click to rename it), and a spine dragged onto another theme's columns, or given one in its tools, goes under that theme for everyone. It follows where you are and lets go when you move it ("Follow" brings it back; so does your next thought after a quiet moment). Far out, words become bars and titles keep their size. Anything can be dragged anywhere and stays there for everyone (`positions`); its pin puts it back. The shelf, bottom right, keeps things at hand for this viewer (in this browser); something new out of view shows as an arrow at the edge.
- `keys` (input, off, demo-safe): the map from the keyboard: j/↓ and k/↑ walk a thread's points (focused on the map), l/→ and h/← the threads in the order they were made; Enter shows a point's details, Esc lets go, ? shows the keys (also on first use), ⌘D (Ctrl+D off a Mac) starts or stops listening. Keys typed in a field stay the field's; the Palace and view bricks keep their arrows.
- `tiny-transcript` (panel, off, demo-safe): your words as you say them (`map.partial`), 11 px on one line inside the composer's box where its "Or type a thought" sits (in the status line, `data-slot="status"`, once one is flush at the window's foot; just above the box while you've typed something in it), the newest three words a shade stronger and in view; while it's on, the dock's big live line stays out (`showsLiveWords`). At a turn's end its words wait for its thought (1.5 s at most), so they turn into it where they were. The first time, a hint says what it is ("Your words as you speak, kept small; slide to resize"), with a labelled size slider (Text size, 9 to 16 px, kept), until a click outside or Esc.
- `live-outline` (panel, off): your current talk as an outline forming while you speak, at the bottom left above the clock and the countdown card, growing upward from its live line (your words as heard, in italic; `showsLiveWords`, so the dock's own stays out). The main line, the thread with most of this talk's thoughts, runs flush left; conversation is a stack: a thought in a new thread steps in one level (three at most), in its room's color and with its name on a chip, and one in a thread already on the way in steps back out to that thread's level. Filler (um, uh, "you know", "like,") is trimmed, never reworded; Enter or a click shows a point's original words, the Original words switch all of them. Double-click or ↗ opens the thread. Two quiet minutes start a new talk. Under 600 px it keeps the last five points.
- `work-spine` (card, off, demo-safe; `?work-spine`): what the AI helpers are doing, as jobs on a spine of their own beside your thoughts. Each job is a bubble, oldest at the top and the newest at the foot with "Just now" on the one that changed last: running (a pulse and a clock), done (✓, how long, what it found), failed (✕, why), waiting (the question it holds, in amber). Side jobs (a `parent`) fold under it as "+3 side jobs", with what in them isn't over beside it; a click or a key opens them, and the open fold reads "Hide side jobs". A waiting job's words wear the question's amber bar only when it is the one that asks: a parent that waits only because a side job does shows its summary ("Class found · card drafted · waiting on your answer") as plain text, and "waiting" counts the questions, not the summaries above them. "Demo mode · simulated" heads it while any job shown is simulated. In the new shell on a window 1000 px or wider it's a lane at the page's right that the Canvas gives room to (`--room-right`, shell.css): the Canvas ends where it begins, so nothing is under it. In a call it starts below the Talk pill, and while Talk's own rail is open (two people here) the lane gives way to the card. Anywhere narrower, or on today's page, it's a card above the composer (the badge, the name, the counts on one line) that opens the list as a sheet above the dock, so the composer is never covered; Esc, the card or a press elsewhere puts it away. It follows the newest while you're at the foot of the list and stays where you scrolled to otherwise ("↓ Latest" brings you back). Nothing is asked of the core: the jobs arrive as the helpers send them, a simulated feed's included.
- `_template` (card): the brick to copy.

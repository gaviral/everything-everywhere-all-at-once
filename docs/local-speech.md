# Local speech interruption

Local interruption uses the existing Conversation partner switch, off by default. The separate Built-in speech switch controls reply playback when this browser detects a local voice. Starting the mic still requires a gesture. With Conversation partner off, playback retains the hard mute and manual cancellation.

Only browser/OS voices reporting `localService === true` receive reply text. Remote and unknown-locality voices are excluded before language or quality ranking. Without a detected local voice, replies stay visible as text, playback controls stay off, and capture does not wait for audio; a later local voice enables future replies without replaying old ones. A failed local voice also falls back quietly to text. There is no remote TTS fallback. The browser locality flag does not by itself prove network isolation or permission to publish the resulting audio.

The capture worklet examines AEC-processed input locally in 20 ms windows at 24 kHz, after resampling. After the browser reports the voice started, it learns the playback residual for 240 ms. Calibration uses the maximum level of the quietest adjacent pair of 20 ms windows: 40 ms of quiet input can establish the baseline before a sustained correction, without waiting for silence afterward. A single low startup window cannot lower the baseline; steady loud input from playback start still sets a loud baseline. It re-examines the calibration input after the guard, preserving early corrections, and requires 160 ms continuously above both 0.012 RMS and three times the learned residual. The baseline follows non-candidate input and freezes during a candidate. For input above the energy gate, normalized autocorrelation at seven lags compares signal shape with the quiet calibration reference. Matching shape within 0.12 at every lag, at up to ten times the calibration RMS, is treated as a playback gain change. New shape can interrupt at the existing energy threshold; much stronger input can override the shape guard. The reference stays fixed for that playback generation so a rising echo level cannot progressively teach the guard a correction. This rejects the tested variable-volume tonal residual as well as steady residual and brief transients. Web Speech exposes no playback PCM reference: this heuristic cannot reject arbitrary changing speaker leakage, distinguish another nearby voice from the mic's owner, or reliably detect quiet speech with the same shape as the residual.

During playback, outgoing 80 ms PCM frames remain zero. A candidate retains 20 ms of preroll plus recent input, locally in the worklet, across pauses up to 100 ms. A pause resets the consecutive confirmation count without losing the first segment; longer pauses discard the candidate. Recent audio rolls within a 480 ms bound, including the partial analysis window. Its generation-tagged notification asks the page to cancel browser speech. Only the current generation, with the switch still on, receives an acknowledgement that releases the onset and subsequent input to the existing transcription callback in order. End, error, timeout, replacement, manual cancellation, opt-out and stale evidence discard the buffer. Pending input expires before exceeding 480 ms; a delayed acknowledgement cannot release it. No audio is persisted. Explicit browser refusal of echo cancellation keeps the hard mute.

## Synthetic verification

- `node scripts/local-barge-check.mjs`: 24/44.1/48 kHz; disabled detector, silence, controlled steady residual levels, short transients, sustained input, retained onset, continuing input, stale acknowledgement, unrelated cancellation/replacement and buffer expiry. Review regressions cover 40/100 ms quiet followed by three seconds of speech, exact once-only PCM retention for both early calibration onsets and 120 ms input / 80 ms pause / 200 ms input, and bounded retention across repeated short syllables. Variable-volume residual cases include 300 ms at amplitude 0.004 followed by 200 ms at 0.025, at 300/600/635/1100 Hz, plus repeated volume changes; a different-shape correction at 0.025 and a same-shape correction at 0.12 remain detectable.
- `node scripts/mic-check.mjs`: existing resampling, echo-mute boundaries, capture permission, gesture, abort and cleanup checks.
- `node scripts/page-speech-check.mjs`: fake voices and capture devices in Chrome; feature changes, voice loading/start, generation validation, AEC refusal and abort. Native offline worklets at all three rates verify variable-volume echo stays muted without cancelling, and run normal, 40/100 ms calibration-contaminating and paused synthetic corrections through detection, the real mic callback, fake speech cancellation and onset release. Existing voice choice, deadlines, manual cancellation and late-frame protections remain covered.
- `cd ui && npm run build`: TypeScript and both production bundles.

## Real speaker and microphone checks

- In Chrome and Safari, on laptop speakers and phone speakerphone, let replies finish without talking at low, normal and high volume, and change the volume during each reply. Confirm no self-interruption, echoed captions, duplicated turns or feedback. Repeat with headphones and in a reflective room.
- Interrupt mid-reply with a normal correction, then a quiet correction and a short “wait”. Confirm playback stops promptly and transcription retains the first consonant and the entire correction once. Try near/far mic distances and normal room noise.
- Try speaking as playback starts and while voices load, then after about 40 ms and 100 ms of quiet input. The 240 ms startup guard and 160 ms consecutive confirmation still limit early/short interruption; input that is loud throughout calibration can still be learned as playback. Assess the missed/late cases and echo response after a quiet playback onset.
- Try clicks, taps, coughs, fans/music and another person talking nearby. Check false interruptions; another sufficiently loud voice can pass this energy gate.
- Interrupt successive replies; also test natural end, Space/Escape, Cancel reply, replacement and disconnect while starting a correction. Confirm capture resumes, no old audio is replayed, and the next reply can be interrupted.
- Switch Conversation partner off during playback, then on again. Confirm off keeps the playback hard mute, on restores interruption, and listening never starts without the mic gesture. Check recovery-command speech as well as conversational replies.

Real acoustic behavior, quiet speech, interruption latency and recognized correction accuracy remain unverified until these trials run.
